# Practice areas

Drafting guidance for the areas a pro se consumer litigant commonly files in. Read the matching
section before drafting; the generic motion and brief skeletons in
`circuit-court.md` still supply the shape.

## 1. Public records enforcement, Wis. Stat. §§ 19.31-19.39

**The vehicle is mandamus.** § 19.37(1)(a): a requester whose request is denied may
"bring an action for mandamus asking a court to order release of the record."
§ 19.37(1)(b) is the alternative route through the AG or the DA.

**The policy declaration is the frame, and it belongs in the introduction.**
§ 19.31: the public records law "shall be construed in every instance with a
presumption of complete public access, consistent with the conduct of governmental
business. The denial of public access generally is contrary to the public interest,
and **only in an exceptional case may access be denied**."

**Core duties.**
- § 19.35(1)(a): "Except as otherwise provided by law, any requester has a right to
  inspect any record."
- § 19.35(4)(a): the authority shall, "as soon as practicable and without delay,"
  either fill the request or notify the requester of a denial "and the reasons
  therefor."
- § 19.35(4)(b): a written denial of a written request must state reasons and must
  inform the requester that the denial is subject to review by mandamus under
  § 19.37(1).

**What the petition must plead:** the requester's status; the request, quoted or
attached, with its date; the authority and the custodian; the response or the
absence of one; why any stated reason fails; and the relief. Plead each request
separately. A petition that lumps requests together invites an order that resolves
none of them.

**The balancing test.** Absent a statutory or common-law exception, the custodian
must apply the balancing test and state the public policy reasons for withholding
**at the time of denial**. Reasons invented later do not count. Key cases:
*Newspapers, Inc. v. Breier*, 89 Wis. 2d 417, 279 N.W.2d 179 (1979);
*Linzmeyer v. Forcey*, 2002 WI 84, 254 Wis. 2d 306, 646 N.W.2d 811;
*Osborn v. Board of Regents*, 2002 WI 83, 254 Wis. 2d 266, 647 N.W.2d 158;
*Journal Times v. Police & Fire Comm'n*, 2015 WI 56, 362 Wis. 2d 577, 866 N.W.2d 563.

**Redaction, not withholding.** Where only part of a record is exempt, the
custodian's duty is to redact and release the rest. Say so expressly, and ask for
partial disclosure in the alternative, it is the relief a court is most likely to
grant.

**In camera review** is the standard mechanism for testing a withholding claim. Move
for it early and frame it narrowly: identify the records, the claimed exemption, and
what the court should look for.

**Mandamus, fees and damages, § 19.37.** If an authority withholds a record or part
of a record, or delays granting access, after a written request for disclosure is
made, § 19.37(1)(a) lets the requester bring an action for mandamus asking a court to
order release of the record. In an action under § 19.37(1) relating to access under
§ 19.35(1)(a), § 19.37(2)(a) directs the court to award reasonable attorney fees,
damages of not less than $100, and other actual costs if the requester prevails in
whole or in substantial part; a committed or incarcerated requester is not entitled
to any minimum amount of damages, but the court may award damages. In an action
relating to an individual's access under § 19.35(1)(am) to personally identifiable
information about that individual, § 19.37(2)(b) directs the court to award the
individual actual damages only if it finds that the authority acted in a willful or
intentional manner. § 19.37(3) adds a punitive damages exposure where an authority
or legal custodian arbitrarily and capriciously denied or delayed response to a
request or charged excessive fees. The
§ 19.37(4) forfeiture of not more than $1,000 is enforced by action on behalf of the
state by the attorney general or the district attorney of any county where a
violation occurs; the requester does not enforce it.

**The fee standard is contested and moving.** *Friends of Frame Park v. City of
Waukesha*, 2022 WI 57, 403 Wis. 2d 1, 976 N.W.2d 263, held that a requester does not
prevail merely by suing and obtaining voluntary release; a judicially sanctioned
change in the parties' legal relationship is required. Later cases work out the
edges: *Wisconsin State Journal v. Blazel*, 2023 WI App 24; *Midwest Environmental
Advocates v. Prehn*, 2025 WI App 55. `[UNVERIFIED]` This skill was told that legislative attempts to undo *Frame Park*
(2025 SB 194 / AB 190) failed in March 2026, and that *Blazel* and *Prehn* work out
the edges. None of that was confirmed. Check the current state of the fee line
yourself. **Check the current state of this line
before briefing fees**: it is the most actively litigated question in the field.

**Pro se and fees.** A self-represented litigant who is not a lawyer generally
cannot recover attorney fees for his own time. Plead whatever § 19.37(2) damages
and costs apply, and the punitive exposure under § 19.37(3), rather than resting the
case on a fee award.

## 2. Small claims, Wis. Stat. ch. 799

**Amount limits, § 799.01(1).** Before January 1, 2027, § 799.01(1)(d) sets a
$10,000 limit for the civil actions specified there, and § 799.01(1)(c) sets a
$10,000 property-value limit for the replevin actions specified there. 2025 Wis.
Act 105, §§ 2, 3 and 5, raises both amounts to $15,000 effective January 1, 2027,
and Act 105, § 4(1), says the change first applies to "actions commenced or claims
made on the effective date of this subsection," that is, January 1, 2027. [VERIFY]
How § 4(1) applies to a claim made before January 1, 2027 in an action commenced
after it is for qualified review. The
**$5,000** limit for third-party complaints, personal injury claims, and actions
based in tort, § 799.01(1)(cr), is unchanged by Act 105. These are not limits on
every chapter 799 proceeding: covered Consumer Act replevin is conducted under
chapter 799 notwithstanding § 799.01(1)(c) and the value of the collateral or leased
goods, § 425.205(1). Section 799.01(1) also covers eviction regardless of the rent
claimed, (1)(a); qualifying earnest-money actions regardless of the amount claimed,
(1)(am); forfeiture actions except where a different procedure is prescribed,
(1)(b); and the specified real-property arbitration matters regardless of the amount
of the award, (1)(cm). Apply each category's own conditions. Act 105, § 1, also
raises the § 421.202(6) Consumer Act exclusion thresholds from $25,000 to $50,000
on the same date; that is not a small-claims limit. Read the current § 799.01, its
effective-date notes, and Act 105 before relying on any figure.

**Chapter 799 is the exclusive procedure** for the actions § 799.01(1) lists. It is
not an election.

**Pleading, § 799.06.** Simplified; the standard court forms satisfy it.

**Discovery is available.** § 799.04(1) makes ch. 804 applicable, subject to the
court's control. § 799.209 is the **informal-procedure and relaxed-evidence** rule,
not a discovery limitation: the court conducts proceedings informally and the rules
of evidence do not strictly apply.

**Substitution, § 799.205**, distinct from § 801.58.

**Removal to ordinary civil procedure, § 799.02.** Available on the terms the
statute sets; it is how a case with real discovery needs gets out of the small
claims track.

**Appeals.** A small claims judgment is appealed to the Court of Appeals like any
other final judgment, subject to the § 799.30 exceptions. Small claims decided by a
court commissioner gets de novo review in the circuit court first.

**La Crosse County adds real traps.** See `circuit-court.md` Part 7 for the full
list; the ones that decide cases:
- **Rule 701:** mail service is authorized in lieu of personal **or substituted**
  service **only within La Crosse County**, except in eviction actions. A defendant
  residing outside the county must be served personally or by substituted service,
  **except in eviction actions**. A mailed summons to an out-of-county defendant does
  not satisfy the rule, and that is a service defect worth raising.
- **Rule 704:** the written answer is due by **10:00 a.m. on the return date**, and
  a mailed answer is filed when the clerk receives it, not when it is mailed.
- **Rule 703:** non-appearance on the return day means default or **dismissal with
  prejudice**, and where there is more than one plaintiff or defendant **all of them
  must appear**. A party may appear by proxy under Rule 703A, and **a non-resident
  defendant may appear by answering by mail before the Return Date** — the route that
  matters most to an out-of-county pro se defendant.
- **Rule 706 / 706A:** mediation is mandatory before any trial is scheduled, and the
  **plaintiff** must request it within 10 business days of the return day appearance
  or face dismissal with prejudice.

## 2A. Consumer credit collection cases, La Crosse County

**La Crosse Local Rule 508 (large claims) and Rule 702 (small claims) impose a
county-specific pleading standard on consumer credit complaints, and the rule makes
it a sufficiency standard:**

> "All complaints concerning consumer credit transactions must follow the guidelines
> and format set forth in the Consumer Credit Complaint Checklist document ... The
> headings and information within the consumer credit complaint **must be structured
> in accordance with the arrangement and organization of the Checklist**. The court
> will use this Checklist in its determination of the **sufficiency** of all
> consumer credit complaints."

The Checklist is published by the Clerk of Courts at
`lacrossecounty.org/docs/default-source/clerk-of-courts/consumercreditcomplaintchecklist`.

**Why this matters on both sides of the "v."** A debt buyer's complaint that does
not track the Checklist's headings and organization is, by the rule's own terms,
open to a sufficiency challenge in La Crosse County that would not exist elsewhere
in the state. **Pull the current Checklist and compare it line by line against the
complaint before drafting a motion to dismiss, a motion to strike, or an answer.**
Conversely, a complaint you draft in a consumer credit matter has to follow it.

This sits on top of, not instead of, the statutory framework: Wis. Stat. ch. 421-427
(the Wisconsin Consumer Act), the § 425.109 pleading requirements for a consumer
credit transaction complaint, and the federal FDCPA, 15 U.S.C. § 1692 et seq. Check
§ 425.109 in particular, it has its own itemized complaint content requirements,
and the county Checklist is best read as implementing it.

**What the Checklist actually is (verified 2026-08-29 against the official
document).** It is not a form or a guidance sheet: it is formatted **as a signed
court order** — full caption (STATE OF WISCONSIN / CIRCUIT COURT / LA CROSSE COUNTY,
party block, Case No.), the title *"Sufficiency of Consumer Credit Complaint
Checklist,"* a preamble, the checked items, and a **"BY THE COURT" / Dated / Circuit
Court Judge** signature block. Each item is preceded by a blank check line, and there
are **ten check items, not nine** — venue carries its own check line on p. 2, so the
court treats venue as a checked sufficiency item. The headings, in document order:
**Identify the Transaction · Facts Constituting Default · Amount of Estimated
Recovery · Figures Necessary to Compute · Amount to Cure · Copy of Writings** — then
the conditional subheading *"If there is **collateral** for the debt:"* — **Collateral
Description · Right to Redeem · Estimated Deficiency Claim** — then *"**Venue** for
Consumer Actions (Wis. Stat. § 421.401)"* with the *Kett* void-judgment note.

**Three defects are visible on the face of the Checklist itself**, and a party
quoting it should know them: (1) **a miscitation** — *Rsidue v. Michaud* is printed
as "295 Wis.2d 585, **496 N.W.2d 708** (Ct. App. 2006)," but that N.W.2d cite is
*Household*'s, reused four lines below its own correct appearance; (2) **the "any
assignee" sentence**, which reads verbatim *"The pleading requirements currently do
not apply to a complaint filed by any assignee of the original creditor."* The
quoted text is confirmed; **whether that sentence overstates *Rsidue*'s holding is
`[UNVERIFIED]`** — *Rsidue* was not read, so do not argue the overstatement as
established; and (3) **a bare *Newgard* reference** — *"In Newgard, the reviewing
court decided that account statements showing individual transactions satisfied this
requirement"* — with **no antecedent full citation anywhere in the document**; the
name arrives cold.

*Source: the official La Crosse County Consumer Credit Complaint Checklist PDF,
pp. 1–2, read complete 2026-08-29.*

## 3. Substitution and recusal, they are different things

Confusing the two is the most common error in this area. Say which one you are
invoking in the first sentence.

**Substitution as of right, § 801.58 (civil).** No cause required, no showing, no
argument. The written request must be filed "preceding the hearing of any
preliminary contested matters" and, if by the plaintiff, not later than 60 days
after the summons and complaint are filed or, if by any other party, not later than
60 days after service of a summons and complaint upon that party. § 801.58(1). For
the preliminary-contested-matter condition, the request must precede the judge's
actual hearing of a substantive issue going to the ultimate merits; a scheduled
hearing alone is not enough. *State v. Matthews*, 2021 WI 42, ¶¶ 18, 20, 22. If a new
judge is assigned to the trial, the request must be made within 10 days of receipt
of notice of assignment, or within 24 hours of receipt if the notice comes less than
10 days before trial; if notice comes less than 24 hours before trial, the action
proceeds to trial only on the parties' stipulation that the assigned judge may
preside. § 801.58(1). **One request per party**, and parties united in interest and
pleading together count as a single party, § 801.58(3), except that after an
appellate decision orders a new trial or reverses or modifies the judgment or order
so that further trial-court proceedings are necessary, any party may file a request
within 20 days after the remittitur is filed in the trial court, whether or not it
filed one before the appeal, § 801.58(7). Small claims have their own section,
§ 799.205, with a different post-appeal trigger. For initial timing, § 799.205(1)
requires the request on the return date of the summons or within 10 days after the
case is scheduled for trial, with 10-day, 24-hour and stipulation rules when a new
judge is assigned to the trial. After an appellate decision that requires further
trial-court proceedings, § 799.205(4) allows a request within 20 days after the entry
of the appellate court's judgment or decision, not after the remittitur is filed.
§ 971.20 is the criminal analogue.

**Disqualification, § 757.19(2).** Seven grounds, (a) through (g). (a)-(f) are
objective: prior representation, a financial interest, a relationship, prior
participation as counsel or a material witness. **(2)(g) is the subjective one:**
disqualification is required "[w]hen a judge determines that, for any reason, he or
she cannot, or it appears he or she cannot, act in an impartial manner."

**The (2)(g) trap.** *State v. American TV & Appliance*, 151 Wis. 2d 175, 443
N.W.2d 662 (1989), holds that (2)(g) is **subjective**: the question on review is
only whether the judge made the determination, not whether an objective observer
would think recusal warranted. *State v. Herrmann*, 2015 WI 84, 364 Wis. 2d 336, 867
N.W.2d 772, addresses the due process overlay in a lead opinion (A.W. Bradley, J.);
none of its three writings garnered a majority. *Miller v. Carroll*, 2020 WI 56, ¶ 23.
The later majority in *Miller* applies the *Caperton* standard verbatim, asking
whether there is "a serious risk of actual bias" based on objective and reasonable
perceptions, and reserves that level for the exceptional case with extreme facts.
*Id.* ¶ 24. The constitutional floor is objective, and it is where an objective
argument actually lives. A motion resting on (2)(g)
alone is asking the judge to disqualify himself and is reviewable only for whether
he answered the question. **If the argument is objective, plead the due process
ground, not just (2)(g).**

## 4. Fee waiver, Wis. Stat. § 814.29

The affidavit of indigency and the petition. Forms **CV-410A** (petition) and
**CV-410B** (order). The standard is inability to pay the costs of the action, or
receipt of listed public benefits.

**What it covers:** filing fees and service costs. **What it does not cover:**
transcript costs in every instance, discovery costs, an opponent's costs if the
litigant loses, or the cost of copies from a records custodian.

Under § 801.18(7)(b), a document filed with a § 814.29 waiver petition "shall be
considered filed with the court on the date and time of the original submission if
the waiver is subsequently granted", so a timely filing is not lost while the
petition is pending.

## 5. Discovery motions, Wis. Stat. ch. 804

**Scope and proportionality, § 804.01(2)(a),** as amended by 2017 Wis. Act 235.
Discovery must be relevant **and proportional to the needs of the case**. The
factors track the federal rule. Brief the proportionality factors explicitly; an
opponent's boilerplate "overbroad and unduly burdensome" objection does not engage
them and can be shown not to.

**Motion to compel, § 804.12(1).**
- **§ 804.12(1)(b)** is the operative definition: "an evasive or incomplete answer
  is to be treated as a failure to answer." That, not (1)(c), is the citation for
  the proposition that a non-answer counts as no answer.
- **§ 804.12(1)(c)** is the **expenses** provision: the court shall award the
  movant's reasonable expenses including attorney fees unless the opposition was
  "substantially justified."
- **§ 804.12(2)(a)** is the sanctions ladder for violating an order: facts taken as
  established, claims or defenses stricken, pleadings dismissed, default judgment,
  contempt.
- **§ 804.01(3)(a)** is the **protective order** provision. Wisconsin has **no
  codified privilege-log requirement.** Ask for a log as relief; do not cite a rule
  that does not exist. The nearest provisions are § 804.01(7) (clawback) and
  § 804.01(2)(e)1r. (ESI conferral).

**Meet and confer, the honest answer.** Wisconsin has **no statewide statutory
meet-and-confer prerequisite** to a motion to compel. There is no analogue to Fed.
R. Civ. P. 37(a)(1). The obligation exists only:
1. by statute for **ESI**, § 804.01(2)(e)1r.;
2. under the **Commercial Court Standard Discovery Protocol**, which binds only
   commercial-docket cases but is the best statement of the expected norm; and
3. by local rule in some counties. Trempealeau County has none.

**Draft the conferral paragraph anyway.** It costs nothing, it is what the
"substantially justified" analysis under § 804.12(1)(c) turns on in practice, and
it forecloses the opponent's easiest response.

**Structure of a motion to compel.** Motion identifying each request at issue by
number → conferral paragraph → then, request by request: the request as served, the
response verbatim, why it is a failure to answer under § 804.12(1)(b), and why the
material is relevant and proportional under § 804.01(2)(a) walking the factors →
answer to each objection, distinguishing a timely, specific objection permitted
by §§ 804.08(1)(b) and 804.09(2)(b)1 from a total failure to serve answers or
objections under § 804.12(4) → relief: an order compelling by a date certain,
expenses under § 804.12(1)(c), a privilege log, and notice that continued
non-compliance draws a § 804.12(2)(a) motion → supporting declaration attaching the
requests, the responses, and the conferral correspondence → proposed order.

## 6. Frivolousness, § 802.05(3) and § 895.044

**§ 802.05(3)(a)1. safe harbor, and it is strict:**

> "A motion for sanctions under this rule shall be **made separately from other
> motions or requests** and shall describe the specific conduct alleged to violate
> sub. (2)."

> "The motion shall be served as provided in s. 801.14, but **shall not be filed
> with or presented to the court unless, within 21 days after service of the
> motion** ... the challenged paper, claim, defense, contention, allegation, or
> denial **is not withdrawn or appropriately corrected**."

So: serve it alone, allow 21 days or another period prescribed by the court,
apply the counting rules, and check whether the challenged conduct was withdrawn
or appropriately corrected before filing. A sanctions request buried in an
opposition brief is procedurally dead, and saying so is a complete answer to one
served that way.

**§ 895.044** is the separate damages statute for frivolous claims and defenses,
with its own procedure. The two do not merge; pick the one you mean.

## 7. Proposed jury instructions and special verdicts

Start with the current [Wis JI-Civil catalog](https://wilawlibrary.gov/jury/civil/)
and [release history](https://wilawlibrary.gov/jury/civil/updates.html). Check the
individual instruction's revision date, comments, statutory basis, party type,
elements, defenses, and burden of proof; a compilation's date is not the revision
date of every instruction. These are models that may require adaptation to the
actual pleaded claim, evidence, current law, and court's pretrial order.

For § 100.18, the catalog checked September 20, 2026 distinguishes **2418A** for
private-party claims under (11)(b)2 from **2418B** for claims brought by the State.
Do not use the old 2418 number or assume those two instructions are interchangeable.
Open the instruction itself before quoting its elements or selecting a burden.

For a claim with no suitable current model, prepare a clearly labeled custom
instruction with supporting authority for each element and burden. Mark changes
to model language, align the proposed verdict questions with the issues to be
submitted, and identify disputed alternatives for the court. Do not invent an
instruction number or declare that none exists without searching the catalog.
