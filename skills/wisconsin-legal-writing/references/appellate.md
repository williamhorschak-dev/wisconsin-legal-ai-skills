# Wisconsin Court of Appeals briefs

Appellate formatting is mandatory and enforced. Rule 809.19 governs briefs;
Rule 809.81 governs every other paper. Verify current text before filing , 
subsection numbering changed in 2021 and many form books still recite the old
version.

**Which district:** La Crosse, Monroe, Vernon, Jackson → **District IV (Madison)**.
Trempealeau, Buffalo, Eau Claire → **District III (Wausau)**. Wis. Stat. § 752.11(1).

---

## Getting to the Court of Appeals

**Notice of appeal, Rule 809.10(1).** File with the **clerk of the circuit
court**. Use the current rule or official CA-120 form for every required item,
including the case name and number, the judgment or order and its entry date,
single-judge/preference status, and any case-specific items in (1)(b).

File a required docketing statement **in circuit court with the notice**;
Rule 809.10(1)(d) exempts several categories, including cases in which a party
represents themself. The statement on transcript also goes to circuit court,
ordinarily within 14 days under Rule 809.11(4)(b); special appeal tracks differ.
The circuit clerk transmits the initiating documents. Serve paper parties by
traditional methods; circuit eFiling supplies service on participating users.
Pay the appellate filing fee through the court's prescribed payment route.
Do not send a duplicate notice and docketing statement directly to the Court of
Appeals as an extra filing step.

Sources checked 2026-09-20: [current ch. 809](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/statutes/statutes/809.pdf),
Rules 809.10-.11, and the Court's [appellate eFiling FAQ](https://www.wicourts.gov/ecourts/efileappellate/faq.htm).

**Rule 809.10(1)(e): "The filing of a timely notice of appeal is necessary to give
the court jurisdiction over the appeal."** Ordinary civil appeal deadlines cannot
be enlarged. Rule 809.82(2)(b) has exceptions for appeals under Rules 809.107,
809.30, and 809.32; identify the track before applying a deadline or extension rule.

**The ordinary civil clock, § 808.04(1).** 90 days after entry of the judgment or order; **45
days** if written notice of entry was given within 21 days after entry
(§ 806.06(5)).

**Cross-appeal, Rule 809.10(2)(b).** The later of the ordinary notice-of-appeal
deadline or **30 days after filing of the notice of appeal**. Compute the actual
dates under the governing track and counting rules.

**Scope, Rule 809.10(4).** An appeal from a final judgment or order brings up all
prior nonfinal judgments, orders, and rulings adverse to the appellant and
favorable to the respondent, not previously appealed and ruled upon.

## Appellant's brief, required contents, in this order

Rule 809.19(1)(a)–(f). Filed within **40 days** of the filing of the record.

**(a) Table of contents and table of authorities.** One paragraph of the rule
covers both. The table of contents carries page references for each portion of the
brief, **including the headings of each section of the argument**: which is why
point headings must be written as propositions. The table of authorities is
(i) cases, arranged **alphabetically**; (ii) statutes; (iii) other authorities , 
each with the pages on which it is cited.

**(b) Statement of the issues.** The issues presented for review **and how the
trial court decided them**. Both halves are required.

**(c) Statement on oral argument and publication.** Whether oral argument is
necessary, **with reasons**; and whether the opinion should be published and, if
so, why.

**(d) Statement of the case.** Four mandatory components:
1. a description of the nature of the case;
2. the procedural status leading up to the appeal;
3. the disposition in the trial court;
4. a statement of facts relevant to the issues presented, **with appropriate
   references to the record**.

**(e) Argument.** Arranged **in the order of the statement of issues**. The
argument on each issue **must be preceded by a one-sentence summary of the
argument**. Citations to authority, statutes, and the record in the form of the
Uniform System of Citation and SCR 80.02.

**(f) Conclusion.** "A short conclusion stating the **precise relief sought**."

**Also apply (1)(g)-(i).** These are requirements beyond the section skeleton:
protect identities in confidential records and under Rule 809.86; use only
"petitioner" where the specified protective-order/harassment-injunction caption
substitutes that designation; obtain the proper attorney or party signature; and,
when an attorney actually supplied limited drafting assistance under § 802.05(2m),
include the statement required by (1)(h). In the argument, use party names rather
than generic designations, subject to those privacy requirements. Do not insert a
lawyer-assistance statement solely because an AI helped draft the document.

## Respondent's brief, Rule 809.19(3)

Due the later of 30 days after service of the appellant's brief (+3 if by mail),
30 days after it was filed, or 30 days after the record was filed.

Conforms to (1) **except** that the **statement of issues (1)(b)** and the
**statement of the case (1)(d)** may be excluded. Everything else stays: table of
contents and authorities, statement on oral argument and publication, argument with
a one-sentence summary preceding each issue, and a conclusion stating precise
relief. A "Supplemental Statement of Facts" where the respondent disputes the
appellant's account is permitted, not required.

Rule 809.19(3)(b) allows a **supplemental appendix** where the appellant's is
inadequate.

---

## Reply brief, Rule 809.19(4)

Due the later of 15 days after service of the respondent's brief (+3 if by mail)
or 15 days after it was filed.

The appellant must file **either a reply brief or a statement that a reply brief
will not be filed.** Silence is a rule violation.

A reply brief must comply with § 809.19(1)(e) and (f); if it cites an unpublished
opinion under § 809.23(3)(a) or (b), a copy must be provided in an appendix to that
reply brief. § 809.19(4)(b).

## Appendix, Rule 809.19(2)

Filed as **a single document separate from the brief**. **Rule 809.19(2)(ae)**. In
eFiling it uploads as its own document; do not merge it into the brief PDF.

Contents, Rule 809.19(2)(a). **Supreme Court Order 20-07 (2021 WI 37) repealed and
recreated (2)(a), created (2)(ae) Form and (2)(am) Confidentiality, and repealed the
former (2)(b). There is no § 809.19(2)(b) or (2)(c).** The current paragraphs are
(a), (ae), (am), and nothing else:
- a table of contents;
- the findings or opinion of the circuit court, **including oral or written rulings
  or decisions showing the circuit court's reasoning** on the issues raised;
- limited portions of the record essential to an understanding of the issues;
- copies of any unpublished opinions cited;
- **`[MANDATORY RULE]` a table of contents giving, for each record item, the title,
  the appendix page on which it begins, and the CIRCUIT COURT DOCUMENT NUMBER.**
  Rule 809.19(2)(a), as created by SCO 20-07. A table listing title and page but
  omitting the document number does not comply, and the (8g)(b) certification
  affirmatively certifies compliance with (2)(a). The table must also give the
  citation of any unpublished opinion included.
- in a judicial review proceeding, the agency's findings and final decision.

**Rule 809.19(2)(am): confidentiality.** Where the record is confidential by law,
reproduce using initials or an appropriate pseudonym instead of full names , 
specifically including juveniles and their parents, **with a notation that the
portions have been so reproduced.**

The single most common sanctioned defect is an appendix that omits the circuit
court's reasoning while the certification says it contains it. Rule 809.83(2).

## Certifications, Rule 809.19(8g)

The certification is at **(8g)**, not the old (8)(d).

**(8g)(a): form and length:**

> I hereby certify that this brief conforms to the rules contained in
> s. 809.19(8)(b), (bm), and (c) for a brief. The length of this brief is ___
> [pages] [words].

Note the cross-reference includes **(bm)**: pagination. Any form reciting
"s. 809.19(8)(b) and (c) for a brief and appendix produced with a [monospaced]
[proportional serif] font" is the superseded pre-2021 version.

The word count may be the count reported by a commercially available word
processor, and **must include words in text rendered as images**.

**(8g)(b)1: appendix certification** (headed CERTIFICATION BY ATTORNEY). Abridged
excerpt, not a complete certification: § 809.19(8g)(b)1 supplies a three-paragraph
form for appellant's counsel that also includes the administrative-review and
confidentiality certifications; use the full form where required. The first
paragraph reads:

> I hereby certify that filed with this brief is an appendix that complies with
> s. 809.19(2)(a) and that contains, at a minimum: (1) a table of contents; (2) the
> findings or opinion of the circuit court; (3) a copy of any unpublished opinion
> cited under s. 809.23(3)(a) or (b); and (4) portions of the record essential to
> an understanding of the issues raised, including oral or written rulings or
> decisions showing the circuit court's reasoning regarding those issues.

Plus the confidentiality assurance where **Rule 809.19(2)(am)** applies.

**(8g)(c)**: the two certifications may be combined in a single document for
signature. **(8g)(d)**: electronic filers may sign electronically per
§ 809.801(12)(a).

### Self-represented litigants

**Self-represented litigants are not required to file either certification.** The
Court's *Guide to Wisconsin Appellate Procedure for the Self-Represented* is
explicit on this for both the form/length certification and the appendix
certification.

But the exemption is from **certifying**, not from **complying**. A pro se
appellant is still bound by the typeface, margin, pagination, and length limits of
(8)(b), (bm), and (c), and still must file an appendix that satisfies (2)(a).

**The obsolete certification.** Do not include "I hereby certify that I have
submitted an electronic copy of this brief … identical in content and format to the
printed form." Rule 809.19(12) and (13) were **repealed**; electronic filers do not
submit duplicate paper copies. Self-represented paper filers still file a paper
copy and serve paper parties under the current rules. The State Bar's older "Appellate Brief Filing Checklist" still
circulating on wisbar.org recites it, along with obsolete cover colors and paper
copy counts. Do not rely on it.

## Form and length, Rule 809.19(8)

### Typeface and layout, (8)(b)

| | |
|---|---|
| Monospaced font | 10 characters per inch, **double-spaced** |
| Proportional serif, body | **minimum 13 point** |
| Proportional serif, block quotes and footnotes | **11 point**, single-spaced, § 809.19(8)(b)3.c |
| Line spacing, proportional serif | between **1.15 and 1.5** |
| Left and right margins | minimum **1.25″** |
| Top and bottom margins | minimum **1″** |
| Italics/bold | case names and citation elements, headings, emphasis |

**Note the conflict with default style.** Circuit court filings use 12 pt at
1.5 spacing with 1″ side margins. An appellate brief needs **13 pt** body and
**1.25″** side margins. Do not carry the circuit court page setup up on appeal.

The typeface itself does carry over. **Century Schoolbook and Times New Roman are
both proportional serif faces and both appear on the Wisconsin court system's
accepted-font list**, so either satisfies Rule 809.19(8)(b). They are this skill's
default choices, not an exhaustive list of permitted fonts. Preserve another
compliant choice requested by the user.

**(8)(bm): pagination:** centered at the bottom, Arabic numerals, sequential,
**beginning with "1" on the cover page**.

### Length, (8)(c)

| Brief | Monospaced / handwritten | Proportional serif |
|---|---|---|
| Appellant, respondent, guardian ad litem | 50 pages | **11,000 words** |
| Reply | 13 pages | **3,000 words** |
| Nonparty (amicus) | 13 pages | **3,000 words** |

Excluded from the **page** count (Judicial Council Note): table of contents, table
of authorities, statement of issues, statement on oral argument and publication,
appendix, supplemental appendix.

**Cross-appeals, Rule 809.19(6).** Wisconsin has no analogue to FRAP 28.1(e)(2)'s
enlarged cross-appeal limits. Neither combined brief gets an enlarged allowance; each
*portion* carries its own ordinary limit under (8)(c), counted separately, and each
cross portion takes its own white cover page with a signature only at its end.
Read § 809.19(6)(b)2. and (6)(c)2. if this ever arises.

## Cover, Rule 809.19(9)

> Each brief or appendix shall have a **white front cover**. The front cover shall
> contain the name of the court, the caption and number of the case, the court and
> judge appealed from, the title of the document, and the name and address of
> counsel filing the document.

**All Court of Appeals covers are white.** The old scheme, blue appellant, red
respondent, gray reply, white appendix, yellow guardian ad litem, green amicus , 
no longer applies in the Court of Appeals.

Cover contents in practice: court name and district; case caption exactly as
issued in the official notice; appeal number; document title (`BRIEF OF
APPELLANT`); circuit court case number and the name of the circuit court judge
appealed from; and the filer's name, address, and telephone number (plus State Bar
number for counsel).

## Form of other appellate papers, Rule 809.81

Motions, responses, petitions, everything that is not a brief:

| § | Requirement |
|---|---|
| 809.81(1) | formatted to fit 8.5″ × 11″ |
| 809.81(3) | monospaced or proportional serif font |
| 809.81(4) | **double-spaced**; minimum 1.25″ right and left margins; minimum 1″ top and bottom |
| 809.81(5) | paginated at the center of the bottom margin, Arabic numerals, starting at "1" on the first page |
| 809.81(6) | clear black image on white background; no carbon copies |
| 809.81(7) | pages secured at the top left corner (paper filings) |
| 809.81(9) | caption: full circuit court name of each party, designating each party's status in both the circuit court and the appellate court |

Note that Rule 809.81(4) requires **double spacing** for non-brief papers, while a
proportional-serif brief runs at 1.15–1.5 under Rule 809.19(8)(b).

  those groups may register voluntarily, § 809.801(3)(b); except as otherwise

## Getting real briefs to model

E-filed Court of Appeals briefs are static PDFs at
`https://acefiling.wicourts.gov/document/eFiled/{APPEAL_NO}/{DOC_ID}` and are
search-indexed. The WSCCA document search at `wscca.wicourts.gov/document-search`
must be typed by hand; it rejects programmatically filled fields.

What good filed briefs do, from three retrieved examples: the cover is a centered
stack, court name / `COURT OF APPEALS` / `DISTRICT [I-IV]`, appeal number, caption,
an appeal-from line naming the disposition, county, and judge with `PRESIDING`, then
the document title and the filer's block. Party designations are compound and
hyphenated (`Plaintiff-Respondent`, `Defendant-Appellant`). The table of authorities
divides by source type, Cases / Statutes / Constitutional Provisions, cases
alphabetical with dot leaders, `passim` for heavily cited authorities, and case
names set **plain** there rather than italic. Point headings are full-sentence
propositions in sentence case, nested Roman then A/B/C then 1/2/3.

One retrieved brief writes "abuse of discretion" in its point headings, which is the
wrong phrase in Wisconsin. Filed briefs are not automatically models.

## `[COURT ORDER]` Expedited appeals: check before assuming § 809.19 applies

**A self-represented appellant is excluded from the expedited program**, so for a pro
se appeal the standard § 809.19 requirements apply in full and the rest of this
section is background. The Court of Appeals' *Procedures for Expedited Appeals*
(IOP § VII) makes all appeals eligible **except**, among others, "appeals in which a
party represents himself or herself," no-merit appeals under Rule 809.32, and appeals
under Rules 809.105, 809.107, 809.30, and 809.40(1) outside chs. 48, 51, 55, 938.

For a counseled appeal, this matters. Wis. Stat. Rule 809.17(1) authorizes the
program and provides that a presubmission conference may be required but
"participation in the court's accelerated briefing and decision process is
**voluntary**." Routing is discretionary and follows a conference; there is no
automatic assignment. `[UNVERIFIED]` No formal objection or removal procedure appears
in the IOP; the opt-out rests on the statute's "voluntary" language.

**If the court enters an authorized expedited briefing order, apply its modifications**
on three points. Per *The Expedited Appeals Rules for Briefing* (Wis. Ct. App.,
updated April 2025):

| § 809.19 | In an expedited appeal |
|---|---|
| Deadlines and brief length, (1)-(7) | **Set by the court's order**, not by the rule |
| (1)(a) table of contents and table of authorities | **Not required** in the appellant's, respondent's, or reply brief |
| (1)(c) statement on oral argument and publication | **Not required** in the appellant's or respondent's brief |
| (8g) certifications | Still required, but certify compliance **"as modified by the court's order"** |

**Check the appendix separately.** Section 809.19(2) is within (1) through (7),
so that range cannot support an assertion that the appendix is untouched. Read the
current expedited briefing rules and actual order for any appendix or
certification modifications; otherwise use the ordinary requirements. Do not
infer a waiver from silence.

**Never assume expedited treatment.** Draft to the standard rules and adjust only on
receipt of an actual court order.

## Other appellate filings

### Rule 809.14, motions in the Court of Appeals

**§ 809.14(1):** a motion "must state the order or relief sought and the grounds on
which the motion is based and may include a statement of the position of other
parties as to the granting of the motion. A motion may be supported by a
memorandum." Any other party may respond **within 11 days** after service (5 days in
a § 809.107 appeal, § 809.14(1m)).

A supporting memorandum and an affidavit are both **permissive**, not required. A
motion is not a brief: Rule 809.81 governs its form (double-spaced, 1.25" side
margins), not Rule 809.19(8).

**§ 809.14(2):** a procedural motion may be acted on without a response. A party
adversely affected by a procedural order entered without a chance to respond may
move for reconsideration **within 11 days** after service of the order.

### Rule 809.82, enlargement of time

The general standard is **good cause**, subject to the rule's exceptions.
**§ 809.82(2)(b)** bars enlarging ordinary final-order appeal and cross-appeal
deadlines, but excepts appeals under Rules 809.107, 809.30, and 809.32.
Paragraph (c) requires the specified consent for Rule 809.105 extensions, and
**(2)(e) bars enlarging the Rule 809.24 reconsideration deadline**. The petition
for review deadline under § 808.10 and Rule 809.62 is also not extendable.
Never assume that every deadline other than a notice of appeal can be enlarged.
See the [Court's self-represented guide, pp. 17 and 28](https://www.wicourts.gov/publications/guides/docs/proseappealsguide.pdf).

### Rule 809.24, reconsideration in the Court of Appeals

Filed within **20 days** of the decision; the deadline cannot be enlarged.
Check Rule 809.24(4) for appeal types in which reconsideration is unavailable.
It may not raise arguments not previously
raised; it is for pointing out what the court overlooked or misapprehended, not a
second brief. A response is filed only if the court requests one.

### Rule 809.62, petition for review in the Supreme Court

**30 days** from the Court of Appeals decision, or its decision on a timely
reconsideration motion as provided in § 808.10. This deadline cannot be enlarged.
**35 pages / 8,000
words**, exclusive of the appendix. The first page is a **white cover page** bearing
`Petition for Review`; the response likewise, bearing `Response to Petition for
Review`. Petition and response "shall conform to s. 809.19(8)(b) and (bm) and (8g)
as to form, pagination, and certification."

**§ 809.62(1r) sets the criteria for granting review**: a real-conflict-in-the-law
question, a question of statewide significance, a decision in conflict with
controlling authority, and so on. **Write the petition to the criteria, not to the
merits.** A petition that argues only that the Court of Appeals was wrong is asking
for error correction, which is not what the supreme court does.

### Rules 809.32 and 809.30, brief notes

**Rule 809.32 no-merit reports** are filed by appointed counsel who concludes an
appeal would be frivolous. A self-represented litigant never files one; if one is
filed in your case you have a right to respond and the court must independently
review the record.

**Rule 809.30** is a separate, faster track for criminal and ch. 48/938/51/55/971.17/
980 appeals, with its own deadline chain (notice of intent, transcript request,
postconviction motion, notice of appeal). **Do not apply the ordinary civil
§ 808.04(1) timeline to a case governed by 809.30.** Read the rule.

### Rule 809.83, noncompliance

The court may impose costs, strike a document, or dismiss the appeal for failure to
comply with the rules. The recurring live example is the **appendix certification**:
courts have repeatedly sanctioned filings where the certification says the appendix
contains the circuit court's reasoning and it does not. *State v. Bons*, 2007 WI App
124, is the leading case. Verify the appendix against the certification before
signing it.
