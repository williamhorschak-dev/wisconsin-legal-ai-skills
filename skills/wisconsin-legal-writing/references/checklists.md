# Pre-filing checklists

These are the mechanical checks, organized by document type. For a **finished draft**
being reviewed as a whole, run the five passes in `review.md` instead, which covers
these plus the argument and tone passes.

Distinguish legal requirements from this repository's style defaults. Check the
actual court, county, case type, filing method, signer, rules, and orders first.
The default font, caption geometry, italics, and restrained voice are preferences;
do not label a compliant alternative a filing blocker without a governing rule.

## Every document

- [ ] Caption matches `references/captions.md` geometry exactly, right tab at
      6.5″, three underline rules, Century Schoolbook or Times New Roman 12 pt, title centered bold 14 pt.
- [ ] Court, county, case number, and any required branch designation match the
      supplied docket and applicable form or order. Do not copy a sample county.
- [ ] Case number in default form (`2025-CV-000123`).
- [ ] Party designations match the case type (Plaintiff/Defendant vs.
      Petitioner/Respondent).
- [ ] Every statute cited was checked against the current biennial edition, and the
      edition is stated once.
- [ ] Every case citation carries the public domain cite first if post-2000, and
      pinpoints by `¶` not page.
- [ ] **Bluebook form throughout**, with SCR 80.02 controlling Wisconsin case
      cites where the two differ.
- [ ] **Every case name is italicized in the body**, in full and short form,
      including the `v.` Signals, `Id.`, `supra`, `In re`, and `ex rel.` are
      italic too. Statutes, reporter cites, and court-and-year parentheticals stay
      roman. In an appellate table of authorities, case names are plain.
- [ ] If the document was built from a spec, every case name in a body block is
      wrapped in `*asterisks*`: an unmarked name comes out roman.
- [ ] Every unpublished opinion cited is authored, post-July-2009, cited only for
      persuasive value, and a copy is filed and served. Rule 809.23(3).
- [ ] Every record reference has a document number.
- [ ] In a circuit court filing, protected information (a social security, employer or
      taxpayer identification, financial account, driver license or passport number) is
      omitted from original documents and redacted from the publicly filed copy of an
      exhibit; information that is required by law or necessary to the action goes
      through the protected information form, including an unredacted attachment when
      redaction is impracticable. § 801.19(2)(c)1.-3.
- [ ] Signature block carries name, address, email, and phone. § 802.05(1).
- [ ] Signature fits the filing method: electronic signature or imaged handwritten
      signature for eFiling; handwritten signature for a paper party. § 801.18(12).
- [ ] That line is **italic**; the typed name beneath it is not. Nothing else in
      the signature block is italicized.
- [ ] No em dashes.
- [ ] Deadline calculation records the governing rule, triggering event, service
      method/time, counting rule, closure/holiday adjustment, and any order or stay.
      Apply § 801.15(5)'s +3 mail or +1 after-5-p.m. electronic-service adjustment
      according to the governing rule and case law, not automatically to an
      entry-based or filing-based deadline. For motion-hearing notice by mail,
      *Strook*, ¶ 26 n.8, applies the mail addition; see `circuit-court.md` § 6.
- [ ] Blank ½″ top margin on every page; blank **2″ × 2″ square top right of page
      1**, clear of the caption. The builder does this; a hand-built caption whose
      county text sits at the top right of page 1 violates it.
- [ ] No document security, macros, or digital signatures in the PDF.
- [ ] Under the size cap: 50 MB circuit court, 20 MB appellate.

## Motion

- [ ] Title names the **party** and the **type of relief**. § 802.01(2)(d).
- [ ] Governing decision standard stated; use an appellate standard of review only
      in a proceeding that calls for review. Preserve accurate quotations.
- [ ] Local rules checked: the county's rules, the § 802.10 scheduling order, and
      the judge's standing order. Trempealeau Rule 6 requires numbered proposed
      findings of fact with record cites on summary judgment; La Crosse Rule 502
      defers to § 802.08 and the scheduling order instead.
- [ ] Opening paragraph states the grounds **with particularity** and **the relief
      or order sought**. § 802.01(2)(a).
- [ ] Statutory hook cited in the opening paragraph.
- [ ] Multiple forms of relief broken into numbered paragraphs.
- [ ] The brief is referenced and actually filed contemporaneously.
- [ ] When required, notice of hearing served with the motion at least 5 days
      before the hearing under § 801.15(4), unless another statute or order controls;
      apply the short-period counting rules and the mail addition addressed in
      *Strook*. **Exception: a Trempealeau County summary
      judgment motion is decided without oral argument unless the court directs
      otherwise (Rule 6.VI), so do not notice a hearing on one.**
- [ ] **La Crosse only:** the notice states the length of time the court allotted,
      obtained from the judge's judicial assistant beforehand. Without it, Local
      Rule 435 says the matter will not be heard.
- [ ] Supporting affidavits served **with** the motion. § 801.15(4).
- [ ] Papers the motion is founded on are attached, except those already filed,
      which are referred to by reference. § 802.01(2)(b).

## Brief

- [ ] INTRODUCTION states the dispute and the relief in its first two paragraphs.
- [ ] Facts carry record cites.
- [ ] Point headings are full-sentence propositions, not labels.
- [ ] Argument sections follow rule → sub-rule → application → counter-argument.
- [ ] Standard of review or burden stated once, not repeated.
- [ ] Adverse authority addressed in the body.
- [ ] CONCLUSION's WHEREFORE matches the relief in the motion word for word.
- [ ] Length checked against the county's local rules and the scheduling order , 
      there is no statutory circuit court page limit, but local limits bind eFiled
      documents. § 801.18(8)(c).

## Discovery response

- [ ] Opening `NOW COMES` paragraph identifies the responding party and the exact
      discovery set being answered.
- [ ] Each request reproduced verbatim before its answer.
- [ ] Every objection states a specific reason, not just a string of adjectives.
      An objection that recites "vague, ambiguous, overbroad, compound, and unduly
      burdensome" and stops is the boilerplate this skill teaches you to attack
      when the other side files it. Name the defect in the request.
- [ ] Each timely objection gives a specific reason and identifies its scope.
      Answer unobjectionable portions as required. §§ 804.08(1)(b), 804.09(2)(b)1.
      Distinguish a valid objection from a total failure to serve answers or
      objections under § 804.12(4); do not require a protective motion for every
      objected-to answer.
- [ ] Interrogatory answers are verified by the person making them; a represented
      party's attorney signs objections. A self-represented party does not need a
      second attorney signer.
- [ ] Supplementation reservation included where the investigation is ongoing.
- [ ] Certificate of service filed if any party is served by mail.

## Declaration (default) / affidavit

- [ ] An eligible § 887.015 declaration may be used without a notary; a sworn
      affidavit may also be chosen. Check territorial scope, exclusions, and any
      required medium. Excluded proceedings may require a specialized form or oath,
      not this template's generic affidavit.
- [ ] Uses the § 887.015 statutory form, with actual signature, date, and
      **city or other location, and state or country**.
- [ ] Personal knowledge stated in paragraph 1.
- [ ] One fact per numbered paragraph; no argument, no legal conclusions.
- [ ] Every exhibit referenced is attached and identified as a true and correct
      copy.
- [ ] Facts would be admissible in evidence. § 802.08(3).
- [ ] If an affidavit is used, the affiant actually takes the oath and signs in
      accordance with applicable notarial requirements. A qualifying § 887.015
      declaration supporting summary judgment does not need a notary.

## Proposed order

- [ ] Filed as **.docx**, not PDF.
- [ ] Blank **3-inch** top margin on page 1; ½″ after.
- [ ] **No** judicial signature block, no `BY THE COURT`, no date line.
- [ ] Title states the outcome (`ORDER GRANTING …`).
- [ ] Recital paragraph identifies the motion by document number and states the
      finding (`The Court finds good cause shown.`).
- [ ] Each ordered item is separately stated and docketable, real dates, not
      "promptly."
- [ ] Every ordered item traces to relief actually requested in the motion.

## Letter to the court

- [ ] No caption.
- [ ] `Re:` line with the short case number.
- [ ] Purpose stated in the first sentence.
- [ ] Asks for nothing that requires an order. If it does, it should be a motion.
- [ ] Copied to every other party.
- [ ] Signed using the method in `signature-blocks.md`, with actual signer and
      required contact information; do not use typed `/s/` alone for a paper party.

## Appellate brief, appellant

- [ ] White cover with court name, district, caption as issued in the official
      notice, appeal number, document title, circuit court case number, circuit
      court judge's name, and filer's name/address/phone. Rule 809.19(9).
- [ ] Sections in Rule 809.19(1) order: (a) table of contents + table of
      authorities → (b) statement of issues **and how the trial court decided
      them** → (c) statement on oral argument **with reasons** and publication →
      (d) statement of the case (nature, procedural status, disposition, facts with
      record cites) → (e) argument → (f) conclusion stating **precise relief**.
- [ ] Paragraphs (1)(g)-(i) also checked: protected identities, the statement for
      actual limited lawyer assistance when applicable, and party-name conventions.
- [ ] Argument arranged in the order of the statement of issues.
- [ ] **Each issue's argument preceded by a one-sentence summary.**
      Rule 809.19(1)(e). This is the most commonly missed requirement.
- [ ] Table of contents includes the argument headings.
- [ ] Table of authorities: cases alphabetical, then statutes, then other
      authorities, each with page references. Case names **plain**, not italic,
      in the table.
- [ ] Body **13 pt** minimum proportional serif; block quotes and footnotes 11 pt;
      line spacing 1.15–1.5; side margins **1.25″**; top/bottom 1″.
      Rule 809.19(8)(b).
- [ ] Paginated centered at the bottom, Arabic, **starting at "1" on the cover**.
      Rule 809.19(8)(bm). Open the PDF and look; a note in the template saying
      pagination starts at 1 is not pagination.
- [ ] Under 11,000 words (50 pages monospaced). Word count includes text rendered
      as images.
- [ ] Appendix filed as a **separate document**, Rule 809.19(2)(ae), with a table of
      contents, the circuit court's findings/opinion **showing its reasoning**,
      essential record portions, and copies of any unpublished opinions cited.
      Rule 809.19(2)(a).
- [ ] **Appendix table of contents gives, for each item, the title, the appendix
      page, and the CIRCUIT COURT DOCUMENT NUMBER.** Rule 809.19(2)(a) as created by
      SCO 20-07. Omitting the document number is non-compliance you would then
      certify to.
- [ ] Expedited appeal? Read the actual order and governing expedited rules for
      authorized changes to ordinary briefing requirements, deadlines, and limits.
      Do not infer that an order waives an appendix or another requirement it does
      not address. See `appellate.md`.
- [ ] Confidential material in the appendix reproduced by initials with a notation
      that it was so reproduced. Rule 809.19(2)(am).
- [ ] Certifications under Rule 809.19(8g)(a) and (b): **unless self-represented,
      which is exempt from certifying but not from complying.**
- [ ] Do **not** include the repealed electronic-copy certification.
- [ ] Brief filed as **text-searchable PDF**; appendix as PDF.
      § 809.801(8)(e).
- [ ] Filed within 40 days of the filing of the record.

## Appellate brief, respondent

- [ ] May omit the statement of issues and the statement of the case. Everything
      else in Rule 809.19(1) is required.
- [ ] Filed the later of 30 days after service of the appellant's brief (+3 if by
      mail), 30 days after it was filed, or 30 days after the record was filed.
- [ ] Supplemental appendix if the appellant's is inadequate. Rule 809.19(3)(b).

## Appellate brief, reply

- [ ] Argument and conclusion only. Rule 809.19(4)(b).
- [ ] Under 3,000 words / 13 pages.
- [ ] Filed the later of 15 days after service or 15 days after filing of the
      respondent's brief. **Or file a statement that no reply will be filed , 
      silence violates the rule.**

## Notice of appeal

- [ ] Filed with the **clerk of the circuit court**, not the Court of Appeals.
      Rule 809.10(1)(a).
- [ ] Contains the circuit court case name and number, the judgment or order
      appealed from, and **the date it was entered**.
- [ ] Required docketing statement filed with the notice in circuit court; a
      self-represented party is exempt from the docketing statement. Statement on
      transcript filed there too under the applicable track (ordinary civil: within
      14 days). Verify fee payment, transmission, and service using Rules 809.10-.11
      and the current appellate eFiling instructions.
- [ ] Track identified before calculating time: the ordinary § 808.04(1) civil
      deadline is 90 days after entry, or **45 days** if qualifying written notice
      of entry was given within 21 days. Other tracks have different deadlines.
- [ ] Timeliness independently verified. Ordinary final-order appeal deadlines
      cannot be enlarged; Rule 809.82(2)(b) excepts Rules 809.107, 809.30, and 809.32.

## Motion to compel

- [ ] Each disputed request addressed under its own heading, with the request and
      the response quoted verbatim.
- [ ] Failure to answer framed under § 804.12(1)(**b**), not (1)(c).
- [ ] Relevance **and proportionality** briefed under § 804.01(2)(a), walking the
      factors rather than asserting the conclusion.
- [ ] Actual conferral described accurately, with supplied dates and substance.
      Check ESI, local, and case-specific conferral requirements; do not invent an
      attempt or assume all cases are exempt.
- [ ] Expenses requested under § 804.12(1)(c).
- [ ] A privilege log requested as **relief**: Wisconsin has no codified
      privilege-log rule to cite.
- [ ] Supporting declaration attaches the requests, the responses, and the
      conferral correspondence.

## Public records mandamus

- [ ] Each request pleaded separately, quoted or attached, with its date.
- [ ] Custodian and authority identified for each.
- [ ] § 19.31 presumption of complete public access quoted in the introduction.
- [ ] Reasons stated at the time of denial identified, and the point made that
      later-invented reasons do not count.
- [ ] Partial disclosure / redaction requested in the alternative.
- [ ] In camera review requested, framed narrowly.
- [ ] § 19.37(2) damages and costs pleaded; the *Frame Park* line re-checked before
      briefing fees.

## Sanctions motion under § 802.05(3)

- [ ] Served alone, **separate from every other motion or request**.
- [ ] Describes the specific conduct alleged to violate § 802.05(2).
- [ ] Served separately and the applicable safe-harbor period allowed before
      filing; § 802.05(3)(a)1 sets 21 days or another period prescribed by the court.
      Confirm counting, correction/withdrawal, and procedural posture.

## Consumer credit complaint or response, La Crosse County

- [ ] Current Consumer Credit Complaint Checklist pulled from the Clerk of Courts
      and compared against the pleading line by line. Local Rules 508 and 702.
- [ ] Headings and organization track the Checklist's arrangement, because the court
      uses it to judge **sufficiency**, not just form.
- [ ] Wis. Stat. § 425.109 content requirements satisfied.
- [ ] Small claims: service method checked against Rule 701. Mail service reaches
      only defendants residing **within** La Crosse County — **except in eviction
      actions**, which are carved out of both the mail-service authorization and the
      non-resident personal-service requirement (verified against the official 2025
      rules, p. 11).

## Proposed jury instructions or special verdict form

- [ ] Pulled from the current Wis JI-Civil compilation (verify its revision date),
      not a superseded archive.
- [ ] **§ 100.18 misrepresentation claim: confirm which instruction the client is.**
      JI 2418 was renumbered in 2/2025 — **2418A is for private-party plaintiffs**
      (§ 100.18(11)(b)2, three elements including pecuniary loss); **2418B is for the
      State** (§ 100.18(1)/(10r), two elements, no pecuniary-loss element). A private
      pro se plaintiff who cites the old JI 2418 or drafts from 2418B is missing an
      element.
- [ ] Burden of proof checked per-instruction: 2418A closes with "(Give Wis JI-Civil
      200)" — the **ordinary** burden — not JI 205's middle burden, which governs
      fraud, undue influence, punitive damages, and acts that would be criminal.
- [ ] Per-instruction revision date checked in the catalog and re-verified against
      the live site before courtroom use — the compilation being current does not
      mean every instruction inside it is.
- [ ] Search the current catalog for the actual claim, including a Wisconsin
      Consumer Act or ch. 427 claim. If no suitable model exists, draft and label a
      custom instruction from current law, with support for every element and
      burden. Do not claim that no model exists without checking.
- [ ] Instruction is not a withdrawn or renumbered entry.
