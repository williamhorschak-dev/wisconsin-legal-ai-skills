# Standards of review

**This file primarily addresses appellate review.** An ordinary trial-level motion
should state the governing decision standard rather than recite an appellate
standard. Circuit courts also conduct review in some proceedings, including
judicial review of administrative decisions and review of court commissioner
decisions; identify that proceeding's governing statute and standard separately.
For ordinary motion standards such as excusable neglect or good cause, consult
`circuit-court.md` and `practice-areas.md`.

A Wisconsin brief states the standard once, near the top of the argument on that
issue, and does not restate it. Get the terminology exactly right; two of these are
places where the wrong phrase marks the writer as an outsider.

## Questions of law, de novo

> Whether [X] is a question of law that we review de novo.

or, where the case came up through the Court of Appeals:

> We review the circuit court's [X] independently, benefiting from the analyses of
> the circuit court and the court of appeals.

*Landis v. Physicians Ins. Co. of Wis.*, 2001 WI 86, 245 Wis. 2d 1, 628 N.W.2d 893;
*Hempel v. City of Baraboo*, 2005 WI 120, 284 Wis. 2d 162, 699 N.W.2d 551.

## Summary judgment

Reviewed de novo, applying the same § 802.08(2) methodology as the circuit court.
*Green Spring Farms v. Kersten*, 136 Wis. 2d 304, 401 N.W.2d 816 (1987).

## Findings of fact, clearly erroneous

**Wis. Stat. § 805.17(2):**

> "Findings of fact shall not be set aside unless clearly erroneous, and due regard
> shall be given to the opportunity of the trial court to judge the credibility of
> the witnesses."

Standard sentence: *We uphold the circuit court's findings of fact unless they are
clearly erroneous. Wis. Stat. § 805.17(2).*

## Discretion, "erroneous exercise of discretion"

**Use "erroneous exercise of discretion" for Wisconsin discretionary review.**
Preserve an accurate quotation or another jurisdiction's terminology when needed.
The supreme court
changed the terminology in *City of Brookfield v. Milwaukee Metropolitan Sewerage
District*, 171 Wis. 2d 400, 491 N.W.2d 484 (1992), and confirmed the change in
*State v. Plymesser*, 172 Wis. 2d 583, 593 n.1, 493 N.W.2d 376 (1992) `[UNVERIFIED pincite; a footnote cite is the easiest kind to get wrong]`:

> "Instead of the phrase 'abuse of discretion,' we now use the phrase 'erroneous
> exercise of discretion.'"

The change was terminological only; the substantive test did not move. The
substantive formulation practitioners use is that a circuit court properly exercises
discretion when it examines the relevant facts, applies a proper standard of law,
and, using a demonstrated rational process, reaches a conclusion a reasonable judge
could reach. That formulation is commonly attributed to *Loy v. Bunderson*, 107
Wis. 2d 400, 320 N.W.2d 175 (1982) and *Hartung v. Hartung*, 102 Wis. 2d 58, 306
N.W.2d 16 (1981), and for evidentiary rulings to *State v. Sullivan*, 216 Wis. 2d
768, 576 N.W.2d 30 (1998). **Pull the pincite and verify the exact wording before
quoting it**: these are widely paraphrased and the paraphrases drift.

## Sufficiency of the evidence

Criminal: *State v. Poellinger*, 153 Wis. 2d 493, 451 N.W.2d 752 (1990); the standard
is the same for direct and circumstantial evidence. Civil: Wis. Stat. § 805.14(1),
no credible evidence to sustain a finding, viewing the evidence most favourably to
the party against whom the motion is made. Read the source before quoting either.

**Confirmed 2026-08-29:** ¶¶44-48 (the paragraphs listed just below) were

*State ex rel. Kalal v. Circuit Court for Dane County*, 2004 WI 58, 271 Wis. 2d 633,
681 N.W.2d 110, is the framework every Wisconsin statutory argument runs through.
The paragraphs worth quoting:

The paragraphs practitioners quote are ¶¶ 44-48 and 51: that the enacted text
binds rather than unenacted intent; that interpretation begins with the language and
stops there if the meaning is plain; that language is read in context, as part of a
whole, in relation to surrounding statutes, and to avoid absurd results and
surplusage; that a statute is ambiguous only if reasonably well-informed persons
could understand it in two or more senses, disagreement not being ambiguity; and
that legislative history is consulted only to resolve an ambiguity or to confirm a
plain-meaning reading.

**Read the paragraphs and quote them from the opinion.** Do not quote *Kalal* from
this file.

## Constitutional questions

Reviewed de novo. A statute is presumed constitutional and the challenger bears the
burden; state the standard of proof the particular challenge carries (beyond a
reasonable doubt for a facial challenge to a statute) rather than leaving it
implicit.
